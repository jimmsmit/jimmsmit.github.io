🔬 Who benefits from corticosteroids in hospitalized patients with Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)?
 
In our recent study published in Lancet Respiratory Medicine, we used data-driven analysis of 8 randomized trials to answer this question. 
Key takeaways:
📈 30-day survival benefits were tied to high baseline CRP levels (>204 mg/L).
⚠️ Patients with lower CRP no mortality benefit from corticosteroids.
 
We further clarify our findings in a newly published correspondence—here’s what we clarified:
 
🔹 Multicollinearities? Low Variance Inflation Factors suggest CRP is not just a stand-in for other observed variables.
🔹 SecondaryOutcomes? 90-day survival and intubation rates also trended toward greater benefit with high CRP.
🔹 BenefitVsHarm? There's a clear trade-off—higher hyperglycaemia and readmission rates in patients treated with steroids highlight the need for careful clinical judgment.
🔹 CRPOnly? While additional markers (like cytokines) may help, our multivariate provided evidence only for CRP as a benefit predictor. 
🔹 SevereCAP? Despite current SCCM guidelines supporting corticosteroid use in severe CAP, we found no greater benefit in severe vs. non-severe CAP—regardless of how severity was defined (PSI, CURB-65, ICU/IMV).
🔹 ViralCAP? In 11% of viral cases (6% influenza), corticosteroids showed a non-significant trend toward harm—even at high CRP—highlighting a need for caution and more research.
🔹 Conclusion? CRP measurement is widely available, routinely used, and therefore an accessible and evidence-supported tool to guide corticosteroid therapy
